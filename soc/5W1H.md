# 5W1H — Scenario 02

## WHO
Resolver-visible affected client: `10.50.30.20`.

No process, user, or malware attribution was proven from DNS telemetry.

## WHAT
Repeated high-volume DNS activity with:
- very high qname uniqueness
- long/highly variable labels
- very high NXDOMAIN ratios
- five consecutive one-minute Detection v1.0 matches
- corresponding ML anomaly classifications

## WHEN
Latest investigated cluster:
- first matching minute: `2026-08-26 06:37:00 UTC`
- last matching minute: `2026-08-26 06:41:00 UTC`

Other matching clusters were also observed in the previous 24 hours.

## WHERE
- Resolver: `dns-soc-resolver01`
- DNS telemetry: `index=dns_soc_dns`, `sourcetype=unbound:dns`
- ML: `index=dns_soc_ml`
- AI: `index=dns_soc_ai`
- Scenario 02 investigation dashboard

## WHY IT WAS SUSPICIOUS / UNUSUAL
The latest burst was far above the client's baseline:
- baseline avg queries/min: 6.11 vs 75–89 in the latest detection
- baseline avg unique qnames/min: 4.12 vs 72–87
- baseline avg NXDOMAIN ratio: 0.158 vs 0.947–1.000

The behavior was therefore strongly anomalous and consistent with DGA-like/high-NXDOMAIN activity.

## HOW
The resolver observed the client repeatedly request many changing DNS names over five consecutive one-minute windows. Most replies were NXDOMAIN.

DNS evidence did not identify the initiating process or prove authorization/malicious intent.
